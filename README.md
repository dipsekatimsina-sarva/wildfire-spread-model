# Modeling Wildfire Spread in Montana

**Program:** Summer Research Early Identification Program (SR-EIP), The Leadership Alliance  
**Institution:** Montana State University  
**Supervisors:** Dr. Elizabeth A. Burroughs and Dr. Mary Carlson  
**Co-researcher:** Esmie Hurd  
**Date:** June - August 2023

Agent-based simulation of wildfire propagation across Montana landscapes, built in NetLogo for the Montana Models mathematics outreach program.

---

## Overview

Wildfires spread at an average of 14.27 miles per hour globally, with spread rates varying significantly based on weather conditions, fuel type, and terrain. As climate change accelerates, wildfires are becoming more frequent and more destructive -- making accurate, accessible modeling tools increasingly important for education, policy, and mitigation planning.

This project developed an interactive NetLogo model simulating wildfire spread across Montana. The model was designed to be hands-on and user-friendly, allowing students to explore how different environmental factors influence fire behavior in real time.

---

## Background

Fire behavior is governed by the fire triangle: heat (which ignites the fire), fuel (which feeds it), and oxygen (which sustains it). While oxygen cannot be controlled, fuel load and landscape features can be managed to reduce spread.

Indigenous communities understood this for centuries -- purposely setting fires to clear shrubs, create safe paths, and protect against threats. Contemporary wildfire science builds on this knowledge, incorporating terrain, vegetation density, and atmospheric conditions into predictive models.

---

## Model Features

The NetLogo simulation incorporated the following environmental variables:

| Feature | Description |
|---------|-------------|
| Forest density | Controls how much burnable fuel exists across the landscape |
| Mountain ranges | Terrain elevation affects wind patterns and fire movement |
| Rivers | Natural firebreaks that can slow or stop spread |
| Wind | Direction and speed influence how quickly fire advances |

Users can adjust these parameters interactively and observe how changes in the landscape alter wildfire spread across the modeled Montana terrain.

---

## Program Context

This research was conducted through the **Montana Models** program at Montana State University, which brings rural youth from across Montana to campus for a summer mathematics and mathematical modeling camp. The wildfire simulation was developed as an educational tool to make abstract modeling concepts tangible and relevant to students living in wildfire-prone regions.

The project was presented as a research poster at the **Leadership Alliance National Symposium (2023)**.
